We are Computational Optics Imaging and Sensing (COIS) Group at both the Beijing and Hangzhou campuses of Beihang University, China. Our research interests are in designing algorithms based optical imaging, microscopy, and sensing systems, including Fluorescent Super-resolution Imaging, Single-pixel/Ghost imaging, Diffuser based Camera, Computational Spectrometer/Hyperspectral Imaging, Fiber Imaging, Wavefront Shaping, Scattering Imaging and Novel Imaging Methods. Innovation in these areas would finds applications in bio-medical, industrial and physical research, which often operates right at the limit of current technology.

    • Research

    Computational Imaging and Sensing in Complex Environments

    This research explores the co-design of optical systems and computational algorithms to efficiently acquire images and multidimensional information across space, spectrum, and time, pushing the resolution and speed limits of conventional imaging. It enables high-resolution, high-speed imaging and precision sensing in complex environments, encompassing single-pixel/ghost imaging, snapshot compressive imaging, ultrafast imaging, imaging through scattering media, and target recognition.

    Selected Publications: Measurement 2026 | Laser & Photonics Reviews 2025 | Photonics Research 2024 | Optics Express 2023 | Optica 2021

    Computational Microscopy and Biomedical Imaging

    Research in this area focuses on developing high-resolution, high-speed, and minimally invasive imaging methods and systems for life sciences and biomedical applications, with an emphasis on computational microscopy, super-resolution fluorescence imaging, and fiber-optic endoscopic imaging.

    Selected Publications: Light: Science & Applications 2025 | Laser & Photonics Reviews 2024 | Nano Letters 2022 | Nanoscale Advances 2022 | Nano Letters 2020

    Intelligent Optics and Novel Imaging Devices

    This research integrates optics, computation, and artificial intelligence to develop miniaturized, integrated imaging and sensing devices tailored to specific tasks. Hardware–software co-design spans information acquisition to target recognition, with research encompassing on-chip multidimensional imaging, computational spectroscopic devices, optical neural networks, and task-specific intelligent sensing.

    Selected Publications: Photonics Research 2026 | Optics and Laser Technology 2026 | Light: Science & Applications 2024 | IEEE Photonics Journal 2021
